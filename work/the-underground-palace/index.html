The Underground Palace

The Underground Palace makes the invisible felt through material, sound, and ritual.

It turns electromagnetic forces into a virtual environment that visitors can approach, interpret, and inhabit.

Role
Experience Designer
Scope
Immersive Installation
Team
Cross-disciplinary Team

Initial Idea

Materialize the invisible so it can become a place.

The project begins with a question: how can forces we normally ignore become perceptible? Everyday objects became points of departure for experiments that gave the unseen a spatial and emotional charge.

Swipe to explore

Narrative Scenario

Turn the invisible into a sequence of ritual encounters.

The narrative unfolds through four scenes rather than one explanation. Visitors move through atmosphere, gesture, and repetition, gradually assembling the meaning of a hidden palace.

Design Development

Build atmosphere through material contrast and spatial scale.

Once the narrative was clear, 3D studies tested how surfaces, openings, and light could make a digital world feel architectural. Sound then became the final layer that gave the palace presence.

3D Modeling

Shape a temple that stays partly hidden.

Spatial studies build a world that feels discovered rather than explained, leaving room for the visitor’s imagination.

Swipe to explore

Sound Design

Let sound make the unseen feel near.

Sound extends the architecture beyond what can be seen, turning distance and absence into part of the ritual.

Final Outcome

An installation that asks visitors to feel before they explain.

The final work combines image, sound, animation, and spatial suggestion into one narrative environment. The palace remains partially hidden, so interpretation becomes part of the experience.

Reflection

Atmosphere can carry meaning when the system leaves room for interpretation.

This project taught me to treat narrative, material, and interaction as one design problem. A coherent atmosphere does not explain everything; it gives visitors enough structure to form their own relationship with what is hidden.
